## The problem

In February 2025 the Three Rivers Festival, Fort Wayne's summer festival for more than fifty years, filed for bankruptcy. Jake and Jes Farris, the brothers behind the Fort Wayne Tattoo Festival, decided the city shouldn't lose its July weekend and announced Big Fort Fest for the same Headwaters Park slot: three nights of national acts, food trucks, a vendor market, and free family programming. Bone Thugs-N-Harmony, Breland, and Theory of a Deadman headlined.

A first-year festival has a first-year problem. The lineup, the schedule, the vendors, and the sponsors all keep changing until the gates open, and the people changing them aren't developers. The app had to ship before the festival existed and keep up with it without a release.

## The build

### A CMS the organizers run

Everything on screen comes from Sanity. Events, participants, festival days, locations, and sponsors are content types, and the home and about screens are singletons the organizers edit directly: which event is featured, which concerts headline the carousel, the FAQ, the sponsor grid. Flags for featured and sort order let them reorder the app from a browser. The Studio lives in the same monorepo with staging and production workspaces, and its schema generates the app's TypeScript types, so a content change that would break a screen fails in the editor, not in someone's pocket at the park.

### Built for the weekend

The schedule opens on today if today is a festival day, grouped into featured, performances, activities, food, and markets. An event has what you'd actually need standing in a field: save it, add it to your calendar, open the stage in Apple or Google Maps, share it as a deep link, and buy tickets by tier in an in-app browser. The directory covers artists, food vendors, market vendors, family-activity hosts, and nonprofits, with search and a type filter.

At launch the app prefetches the home page, the about page, the sponsors, and every day's events, and holds the splash until the home content is loaded. The splash itself is an animated reveal of the logo rather than a static image, because a five-second delay feels shorter when something is moving.

### Updates without a release

Expo Updates, keyed to the app version, meant fixes went out over the air during festival week. The store history tells the story: 1.0 on June 23, 1.0.1 on June 29 with concerts on the home page and a time zone fix, 1.0.2 on July 7 with a featured event section and a new directory category for the family programming. Gates opened July 10.

### Design

Coral, gold, and teal on cream, with Lilita One for headings and Nunito for body text. Playful enough for a festival, legible in sun. Light mode only, on purpose.

## How it went

The app went live two and a half weeks before the inaugural festival and ran through all three nights, July 10 to 12, 2025, at Headwaters Park. The organizers managed every schedule change themselves. Five stars on the App Store, no data collected, and the festival is already planning 2026.

## What it demonstrates

Shipping a complete consumer app on a hard date for a client who had never had one. Choosing a content model so non-technical organizers own the product after launch. And the unglamorous parts: EAS builds for two stores, over-the-air fixes, time zones, calendars, maps, deep links, and a splash screen that hides the network.
